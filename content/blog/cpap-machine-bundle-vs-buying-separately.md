"Bundle" sounds like a discount. For CPAP equipment at CPAP.com, mostly it is not. When we priced the machine-and-mask bundles against their parts, ten of eleven came out at exactly the same total as buying the machine and the mask separately, to the dollar. One cost $35 more.

That changes the question. If a bundle is not cheaper, it is a convenience, and the useful things to ask are what is actually inside the box, what is missing from it, and which parts you were going to buy anyway.

Prices below were taken from CPAP.com's product listings on October 8, 2026. This is a price comparison, not a hands-on test, and prices move.

## The short answer

**For a machine and a mask,** buying the bundle or the parts costs the same at list price, so choose on convenience. One checkout and a mask picked to go with the machine is a perfectly good reason to buy a bundle. Saving money is not.

**Check the arithmetic anyway.** One of the bundles we priced, the AirSense 10 with the AirFit F20, was $35 above the sum of its parts, and the listing mentions no extras to explain it.

**Look at what is missing.** The AirSense 11 bundles ship with a standard hose, not a heated one. The heated hose is a separate $46 on sale ($66 list), and it is the cost people forget.

**For supplies and travel kits,** the answer depends on whether the parts are on sale that week, and in one case a bundle cost more than its parts at current sale prices.

## What that means

**A bundle at CPAP.com is not a way to save money on a machine and mask.** The price is the sum, and the "bundle" is the packaging. Anyone who tells you a bundle is a discount should be able to show you the arithmetic.

**Do the arithmetic yourself,** because the one exception is the kind of thing that happens when two prices drift apart. The AirSense 10 with F20 bundle is $1,150, while the machine ($960) and mask ($155) add up to $1,115. The listing describes no extra items, so on the information we could see there is no reason to pay the $35. If you want that machine and mask, buy them separately.

**Sale prices can change this.** Individual parts get marked down, and a bundle's price does not always follow. That is how the next example came out the other way.

## Where bundles can win, or just be different: travel

The Transcend Micro 510 travel machine is $829. CPAP.com sells it in two bundles that are easy to misread.

The **Essentials bundle** is $988.90. The **Power bundle** is $1,338.85, which is exactly the Essentials bundle plus the PowerAway battery at its current $349.95.

So if you compare the Power bundle with "machine plus battery" ($829 + $349.95 = $1,178.95), the bundle looks $160 more expensive. It is not a markup. That $160 is the Essentials content: a year of replacement supplies and travel accessories, which the listing describes as a travel bag, international plug adapters, a replacement hose, backup filters, and waterless humidification.

Whether that is worth it depends on whether you would buy those things anyway. If you want a year of supplies and the accessories, the bundle is a sensible way to get them. If you only want the machine and the battery, buy those two. Our [travel CPAP comparison](/blog/best-travel-cpap-machines-2026) covers the two machines and the batteries in detail.

The lesson generalizes. When a bundle costs more than the parts you had in mind, check whether it contains more than the parts you had in mind.

## What a bundle does not include

For a home machine this is the real cost question.

**The AirSense 11 bundles come with a SlimLine hose, which is not heated.** The AirSense 11's climate control needs the separately sold ClimateLineAir 11 hose. On sale it is $46 ($66 list), which brings the AirSense 11 with an N20 mask to about $1,155.

**The AirSense 10 includes a heated ClimateLine hose in the box,** and its P10 bundle says so. The same N20 setup is $1,065 as a bundle, about $90 less than the AirSense 11 once you add the hose. That is the gap we describe in [AirSense 11 vs AirSense 10](/blog/resmed-airsense-11-vs-airsense-10), and it is roughly what you pay for the AirSense 11's guided setup and built-in cellular.

**The React Health G3 X includes a heated tube,** so its $972 bundle with a Nova mask is already a heated-hose setup.

The bundle descriptions we read do not list a spare water chamber, spare filters, or extra cushions, which are the parts that run out first. See [when to replace a CPAP machine](/blog/when-to-replace-cpap-machine) for what wears out and when.

## The fit and returns question

The part of a CPAP setup most likely to be wrong the first time is the mask. A bundle asks you to choose one when you buy the machine, before you have slept in it.

CPAP.com's published policies are clear about the two things separately. A machine gets a one-time 60 night risk-free return from the ship date, in original packaging and free of heavy wear, with return shipping at your cost. An opened mask can be exchanged for a different mask or size within 60 nights of purchase.

What the published policy does not say is how either rule applies to a bundle. We could not find any language about whether a mask bought in a bundle gets the fit guarantee, or whether a bundle can be returned whole or split. Ask CPAP.com before you buy if that matters to you, and get the answer in writing.

If you have never worn a CPAP mask, our [guide for a new diagnosis](/blog/best-cpap-machine-for-beginners) covers the three mask types and how to choose, which is worth reading before a bundle makes the choice for you.

## When a bundle makes sense

- **You need a machine and a mask now,** for example after a new diagnosis, and you want one order with the sizes chosen.
- **You were going to buy that exact machine and mask anyway,** and the bundle is priced at the sum of the parts.
- **The bundle is on sale** below its parts. That did not happen in what we checked, but it can.

## When to buy separately

- **You already own a mask you like.** A bundle makes you pay for a second one.
- **You want a mask the bundle does not offer.** The bundles use a handful of masks, and many others are not paired with any machine.
- **A part you need is not in the box,** such as a heated hose for the AirSense 11. You will be buying it separately either way.
- **The parts are on sale** and the bundle is not, as with the supplies example above.
- **The bundle does not itemize its contents,** so you cannot check what you are paying for.

## If you are paying through insurance

Everything above is a cash purchase. If your plan covers a machine and supplies through a durable medical equipment supplier, price that route first, because a bundle bought at retail replaces coverage you may already have. Our [new diagnosis guide](/blog/best-cpap-machine-for-beginners) explains how the Medicare rental and adherence rules work, and most commercial plans follow a similar structure.

## Before you buy any bundle

1. **Find the itemized contents.** If the listing does not say what is in it, treat that as a reason to look elsewhere.
2. **Price each part separately.** It takes two minutes and, as the table shows, occasionally finds a $35 difference.
3. **Check the hose.** Heated or not, and for which machine.
4. **Check the sale banners.** CPAP.com was showing sitewide sale banners while we checked, and we could not tell whether they apply to machines or to bundles. Compare the cart total, not the listed price.
5. **Ask about returns on the bundle specifically,** since the published policy does not address it.
6. **Upload your prescription.** Every legitimate US retailer needs one before a machine ships.

## The bottom line

At CPAP.com a machine-and-mask bundle is priced at the sum of its parts, so it is a convenience and not a discount. Buy one if you want one order and the mask suits you, and skip it if you already own a mask or want a part it does not include.

The money is usually elsewhere: the heated hose the AirSense 11 bundles leave out, the one bundle that costs $35 more than its parts, and the supplies bundle that only wins when nothing in it is on sale. Check the contents, do the arithmetic, and compare the cart.
